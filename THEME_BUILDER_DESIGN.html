1. Neutral Technical
Crisp, restrained, precise. Inspired by Linear and Figma. Emphasizes clarity and hierarchy through weight and structure, not decoration. Cool neutral palette. Sharp edges. Clean 1px dividers. Maximum 8px radius. Monospace for technical values.

Visual Decisions

  • Command bar: Cool #fafafa with sharp action buttons. No rounded container around Save. Status in warning orange (#ea580c) for immediate legibility.
  • Section rail: Subtle light blue (#e0f2fe) for selected state. Text labels with no icon. Quiet neutral gray for unselected.
  • Preview: True isolation via dark background (#1f2937). Toolbar integrated into top edge. Status line sits below content.
  • Inspector: Generous white space. 1px semantic dividers between sections. Accent action rendered as a staged button (not filled).
  • Typography: Clean sans-serif (system stack). 13px for controls. 12px for secondary. 14px for section titles. Monospace for OKLCH only.

Trade-offs

  • Clarity gain: Minimal visual noise maximizes content dominance. Every pixel serves function.
  • Warmth cost: Cool palette may feel clinical in extended use. Requires excellent contrast for approachability.
  • Familiarity: Linear/Figma users recognize this immediately. Reduces learning curve for power users.
  • Constraint: Limited decorative breathing room. Demands disciplined spacing and typography to avoid harshness.
  • Brand fit: Strong alignment with Synapcity's "technical, editorial, calm" positioning. Least decorative, most serious.
2. Editorial Instrument
Typographic and document-oriented. Stronger rhythm and generous spacing. Treats the builder as a serious editorial tool. Softer 10px radius. Warmer neutrals. Pronounced section separators. Ledger-like column alignment with more breathing room between groups.

Visual Decisions

  • Palette: Warm neutrals (#fcfbf9, #fefdfb) instead of stark white. Feels less sterile. Beige undertone without being dated.
  • Selected state: Warm beige (#f5e6d3) with softer border. More inviting than bright highlight. 10px radius for approachability.
  • Dividers: Heavier 2px separators in inspector create strong breathing room. Ledger-like section boundaries.
  • Typography: Bolder section titles (font-weight: 700). Secondary text in italic for subtle hierarchy. More generous leading (24px between sections).
  • Actions: Warmer dark button (#44403c) with 6px radius. "Show all mappings" uses arrow indicator. More editorial tone overall.

Trade-offs

  • Warmth gain: More inviting and calming for extended use. Pairs well with Synapcity's "editorial" positioning.
  • Clarity cost: Slightly reduced contrast vs. pure neutrals. Requires careful color choices to maintain WCAG AA.
  • Density: Heavier dividers and generous spacing reduce information density. Takes slightly more vertical space.
  • Personality: Feels more like a tool for writers/editors than engineers. Strong brand character.
  • Implementation: Requires careful palette tuning. Warm neutrals can easily become dated or muddy if not precise.
3. Warm Technical Ledger
Subtle warmth and material character without skeuomorphism. Soft edges (10px radius). Restrained elevation and soft shadows. Paper-like quality. Warm greys with carefully balanced accent. Combines technical precision with approachable material feel. Feels like a high-end tool, not a spreadsheet.

Visual Decisions

  • Warmth: Warm taupe/caramel undertones (#faf8f3, #6b5b45) create approachability without dated beige aesthetic.
  • Elevation: Restrained drop shadows (1–2px) on buttons and surfaces create subtle depth. No heavy shadowing. Soft material quality.
  • Radius: Consistent 8–10px radius across buttons, inputs, and cards. Softer than flat design but not rounded pill-style.
  • Selected state: Warm accent background (#f1e8d9) with soft shadow. Feels selected without being harsh or neon-bright.
  • Overall tone: Premium ledger or manuscript tool. Technical precision meets handcrafted quality. "High-end creative instrument."

Trade-offs

  • Warmth and approachability: Softens technical austere feel. More inviting over sustained use than pure neutrals.
  • Visual weight: Subtle shadows add perceived depth. Helps regions feel distinct without using borders everywhere.
  • Risk: Warm palette can feel dated or precious if not executed carefully. Requires restraint to avoid "cozy but immature" feel.
  • Contrast: Warm neutrals may reduce contrast. Careful color testing needed to maintain WCAG AA. Darker text required.
  • Brand character: Balances Synapcity's "technical" and "editorial" positioning. Feels like a serious tool made by people with taste.

Recommendation: Warm Technical Ledger (Variant 3)

Warm Technical Ledger best serves Synapcity's positioning as a calm, editorial, technical tool without sacrificing clarity or precision.

Why:

  • • Neutrality with humanity: Warm taupe greys feel less clinical than pure neutrals while remaining neutral-first. The 8–10px radius and restrained shadows convey a mature, well-crafted tool without being decorative.
  • • Brand alignment: "Calm, technical, editorial" personality requires restraint (eliminating Variant 1's coldness) and precision (avoiding Variant 2's decorative weight). Variant 3 splits the difference.
  • • Legibility and contrast: Warm greys with darker text (#2a2420) maintain WCAG AA contrast better than Variant 2's lighter palette. Technical accuracy is never sacrificed.
  • • Extended use: Subtle material feel (soft shadows, paper-like surfaces) reduces eye fatigue over sustained editing sessions. The preview feels properly isolated without aggressive dark/light contrast breaking.
  • • Scalability: Warm Technical Ledger works equally well in light and dark application themes without requiring separate builder variants. The warm undertone feels intentional in both contexts.

Rationale against the other variants:

  • • Variant 1 (Neutral Technical) is precise and familiar but feels austere and uninviting for a "calm" product. Linear/Figma users recognize it immediately, but at the cost of personality.
  • • Variant 2 (Editorial Instrument) succeeds at warmth and character but the heavier dividers, italic secondary text, and bolder typography risk feeling over-designed for a tool focused on user content (the preview) rather than the interface itself.

Proceed with Variant 3, focusing on precise execution: test warm neutral palette (#faf8f3, #fefdfb, #e5ddd0, #9a8f7f) for WCAG contrast, calibrate drop-shadow opacity to avoid muddiness, and ensure 8–10px radius is consistently applied across interactive elements.
